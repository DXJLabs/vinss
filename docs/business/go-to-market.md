# VINSS Go-to-Market

> **VINSS should earn its market one completed deal at a time before trying to manufacture growth with broad campaigns or token incentives.**

**Status:** Go-to-market baseline  
**Scope:** VINSS  
**Evidence date:** 2026-08-29  
**Purpose:** Define how VINSS should find an initial beachhead, acquire real users, move them to a first paid deal, create repeat usage and referrals, and later expand through partners without confusing ecosystem attention with customer validation.

---

## 1. GTM Thesis

VINSS should not begin with:

```text
launch publicly
→ attract as many wallets as possible
→ reward activity
→ hope a market appears
```

The initial motion should be:

```text
find users with a real direct deal
        ↓
understand the current workflow
        ↓
bring that deal into VINSS
        ↓
help both parties reach settlement
        ↓
measure friction + willingness to pay
        ↓
observe repeat usage
        ↓
observe referral
        ↓
identify a recurring segment
```

The governing principle is:

> **The first goal is not maximum signups. The first goal is repeated successful paid deal behavior from a coherent user group.**

---

## 2. What GTM Must Prove

A successful technical launch does not prove a go-to-market motion.

VINSS GTM needs evidence for five separate questions.

### Problem

```text
Does this user repeatedly experience a painful direct-deal problem?
```

### Acquisition

```text
Can VINSS reach similar users repeatedly?
```

### Activation

```text
Can two counterparties move a real deal into VINSS?
```

### Monetization

```text
Will they pay the required fees?
```

### Retention

```text
Will either participant choose VINSS for another deal?
```

The initial GTM system should optimize for learning across all five.

---

## 3. The GTM Unit Is a Deal, Not a Wallet

VINSS is a two-party transaction product.

A wallet connection alone is weak evidence.

A more meaningful funnel is:

```text
User reached
        ↓
real deal identified
        ↓
Private Room created
        ↓
counterparty joins
        ↓
negotiation / Offer
        ↓
Accepted Agreement
        ↓
Rekber funded
        ↓
Fulfillment
        ↓
verification
        ↓
successful settlement
        ↓
repeat or referral
```

The primary GTM unit should therefore be:

```text
Completed eligible deal
```

rather than:

```text
connected wallet
```

or:

```text
page view.
```

---

## 4. Initial ICP Is a Hypothesis

VINSS does not yet have a validated beachhead.

The strongest early-user profile is likely someone who:

```text
already makes direct deals;
negotiates in chat;
uses crypto / stablecoins;
sometimes deals with new counterparties;
has meaningful value at risk;
cares about privacy;
uses manual evidence today;
repeats the workflow.
```

Candidate segments include:

```text
crypto-native freelancers;
digital-goods sellers;
selected bounty relationships;
crypto-native vendors / teams;
direct NFT / asset traders;
OTC participants.
```

GTM should discover which segment pulls hardest.

It should not declare a winner before behavior exists.

---

## 5. Initial Wedge

The best initial wedge is not:

```text
everyone who uses Starknet
```

and not:

```text
everyone who needs escrow.
```

A practical early wedge is:

> **Digitally native bilateral deals where both participants already understand wallets and at least one side has a clear reason not to send payment directly.**

Examples:

```text
freelance task;
digital deliverable;
private design / coding work;
selected bounty;
custom crypto-native vendor deal.
```

Why begin here:

```text
wallet readiness is higher;
Fulfillment is digital;
delivery evidence is easier;
shipping integrations are unnecessary;
the Deal Room workflow is understandable;
repeat behavior is possible.
```

This is a **validation priority**, not a permanent market limitation.

---

## 6. Why Not Start With the Broadest Market

Physical commerce, fiat OTC, and mainstream freelance markets may eventually be large.

They introduce additional adoption problems:

```text
wallet onboarding;
fiat rails;
logistics;
identity expectations;
consumer support;
regulation;
external evidence;
dispute complexity.
```

VINSS should avoid confusing:

```text
large theoretical market
```

with:

```text
best place to learn.
```

The initial market should minimize irrelevant complexity while maximizing exposure to the core VINSS problem.

---

## 7. Founder-Led GTM First

Early VINSS GTM should be founder-led.

That means manually:

```text
finding users;
interviewing them;
watching the workflow;
helping with onboarding;
observing wallet friction;
following the transaction to completion;
asking why they would or would not repeat.
```

This is not inefficient at the earliest stage.

It is the fastest way to learn:

```text
which problem is real;
which terminology users understand;
which steps break;
which fees create resistance;
which users return.
```

Automation should follow repeated patterns.

It should not precede discovery.

---

## 8. Recruit Deals, Not Survey Respondents

A useful customer conversation begins with:

> **Tell me about the last direct deal you completed.**

Better targets are users who can describe:

```text
counterparty source;
negotiation channel;
deal value;
payment rail;
who moved first;
what could go wrong;
how delivery was proven;
whether escrow was used;
what fee was paid;
what happened after settlement.
```

After understanding the past behavior, VINSS can ask:

> **Do you have another similar deal coming up that we can run through VINSS?**

That transition is critical.

The strongest discovery participant can become the first real user.

---

## 9. The Concierge Deal Program

An early acquisition mechanism can be a small **concierge deal program**.

This is not a permanent service.

The purpose is to observe real usage closely.

Process:

```text
qualify user
        ↓
confirm a real upcoming deal
        ↓
help both parties enter VINSS
        ↓
observe without controlling the deal
        ↓
collect friction / confusion
        ↓
follow through settlement
        ↓
post-deal interview
```

The founder should not become:

```text
custodian;
unofficial arbitrator;
counterparty guarantor.
```

The role is product onboarding and research.

---

## 10. Qualification Before Onboarding

Do not spend equal effort on every interested wallet.

A strong early deal has:

```text
two real participants;
real economic value;
specific obligation;
real deadline;
supported asset;
participants willing to use the wallet flow;
no obvious prohibited or illegal activity;
clear enough terms to test the product.
```

A weak test is:

```text
founder sends $1 to friend
just to create activity.
```

That validates execution.

It does not validate the customer job.

## 13. Distinguish Acquisition Failure From Product Failure

Example:

```text
100 people see VINSS
3 start a real deal
```

could indicate:

```text
poor targeting;
weak problem;
weak messaging.
```

But:

```text
30 qualified users start
25 create a room
20 invite
18 accept Offer
4 fund Rekber
```

suggests a different problem:

```text
trust;
pricing;
wallet UX;
settlement explanation.
```

GTM metrics should preserve these distinctions.

## 14. Pricing Is Part of GTM Validation

Current business-planning baseline:

| Action | Public baseline |
| --- | ---: |
| Private Room activation | $0.25 |
| Message | $0.15 |
| Fulfillment | $0.15 |
| Review / Approve | $0.15 |
| Offer / Counter / Accept | $0.25 |
| Rekber | max($0.75, 2% of deal value) |
| Release | Included |
| Claim payment | Included |
| Settlement SBT | Free from VINSS; claimant pays gas |

The actual production quote may increase through the dynamic cost floor.

GTM therefore needs to test:

```text
Do users accept paid messages?

Do users understand transaction-based pricing?

Is 2% Rekber acceptable?

At what deal size does 2% become objectionable?

Do users understand why included actions can still exist inside Rekber economics?

Does the dynamic quote surprise them?
```

Pricing feedback must come from users facing real payment decisions.

---

## 15. Do Not Hide Fees During Validation

Artificially making every early action free can create false demand.

A useful sequence is:

### Technical testing

Subsidize freely when the purpose is technical QA.

### Early customer testing

Show the intended price clearly.

If VINSS chooses to subsidize it, show:

```text
normal price
subsidy
amount user pays today
```

### Business validation

Require real payment.

The question is:

> **Would the user still choose VINSS when the product is not free?**

Its Seed Grants program describes non-dilutive support of up to:

```text

```

for qualifying early-stage teams with an MVP or proof of concept.

The STRK20 Private Sprint also states that strong projects can receive continued support, technical feedback, ecosystem introductions, and a path toward Starknet Foundation grant programs.

This can be valuable for VINSS.

But:

```text
grant received
≠
customer acquired

grant received
≠
willingness to pay

hackathon placement
≠
product-market fit
```

Grant capital should extend runway for:

```text
product improvement;
security;
customer discovery;
distribution experiments.
```

It should not replace product revenue in the long-term business model.

The strongest message maps directly to the recipient's actual workflow.

## 29. Partner Proposition

A future partner proposition should not be:

> Integrate VINSS because privacy is cool.

It should be:

> **Give your users a protected direct-deal workflow without building negotiation, structured Offers, deal-linked Rekber, Fulfillment state, verification, and settlement evidence from scratch.**

Partner value must be measurable through:

```text
new transaction volume;
higher-value P2P use cases;
retention;
fee revenue;
product differentiation.
```

## 30. Integration Before Platform

Do not build a large generic B2B API before a partner asks for repeated capabilities.

The progression should be:

```text
manual partner conversation
        ↓
one concrete integration
        ↓
identify repeated primitives
        ↓
stabilize API / SDK
        ↓
sell infrastructure.
```

Otherwise VINSS risks building an abstraction with no customer.

## 31. Referral Is Native to the Product

A VINSS deal naturally requires:

```text
Participant A
→ invites Participant B
```

This is a powerful distribution property.

The Invite can create a loop:

```text
Alice creates Deal Room
        ↓
Bob joins
        ↓
Bob completes deal
        ↓
Bob now knows VINSS
        ↓
Bob later invites Carol
```

The key metric is not:

```text
Invite links created.
```

It is:

```text
invited user
→ meaningful activity
→ completed Rekber
→ later creates new deal.
```

---

## 32. Loyalty Supports Referral Quality

The loyalty design can reinforce meaningful referrals:

```text
referral joins
→ inviter reward

referral performs first activity
→ additional reward

referral completes first Rekber
→ largest referral reward
```

This intentionally values:

```text
conversion
```

over:

```text
link spam.
```

The referral system should never become the primary reason to use VINSS.

Product demand comes first.

---

## 33. SBT and Reputation as Retention

Successful eligible settlements can create Settlement SBTs.

Potential retention loop:

```text
complete deal
        ↓
claim Settlement SBT
        ↓
reputation history grows
        ↓
loyalty multiplier grows
        ↓
future successful Rekber earns more Points
        ↓
reason to return
```

This is useful only if the underlying deal behavior is genuine.

Do not incentivize:

```text
self-dealing;
wash settlement;
fake counterparties;
refund farming.
```

Future VINSS token distribution should not be the initial acquisition engine.

Avoid positioning such as:

```text

earn token;
airdrop soon;
use app for allocation.
```

This creates demand for:

```text
rewards
```

rather than:

```text

```

The correct hierarchy is:

```text

        ↓
verified Points
        ↓
successful settlement reputation
        ↓
future seasonal allocation if launched.
```

Token demand and product demand should be measured separately.

## 35. Anti-Farming Is a GTM Requirement

A loyalty campaign can corrupt product metrics.

Reward only events that can be verified and deduplicated.

Examples:

```text
message actionLocator;
Offer actionLocator;
Fulfillment actionLocator;
custody commitment;
Settlement Certificate token ID;
referral invite + wallet pair.
```

Analytics should retain:

```text
organic users;
campaign users;
subsidized users;
paid users
```

as separate cohorts.

Otherwise VINSS cannot tell whether growth is genuine.

## 38. Do Things That Do Not Scale — Deliberately

Early examples:

```text
one-on-one onboarding;
watching screen recordings with consent;
direct Telegram support;
manual post-deal interviews;
manual deal qualification;
personal follow-up.
```

The founder should record recurring issues.

Once the same issue appears repeatedly:

```text

write onboarding;
automate support;
improve documentation.
```

Do not permanently hide broken UX behind concierge support.

## 39. Post-Deal Interview

After settlement ask both parties separately:

```text
What would you have used without VINSS?

Which step felt most valuable?

Which step felt unnecessary?

Where were you worried?

Did the fee feel worth it?

Would you use VINSS for your next similar deal?

For what deal would you not use VINSS?

Would you invite another counterparty?

What would make you stop using it?
```

Separate interviews reduce social influence between counterparties.

---

## 40. Retention Before Growth

The key question is:

> **Does a user who has completed one deal initiate or accept another VINSS deal without being pushed?**

Track:

```text
second paid action;
second room;
second counterparty;
second funded Rekber;
second completed Rekber.
```

High signup growth with no repeated deal behavior is weak evidence.

---

## 44. Paid Acquisition Should Come Late

Do not buy large traffic before knowing:

```text
activation rate;
paid conversion;
retention;
contribution margin;
customer lifetime value.
```

Otherwise VINSS can pay to acquire users who:

```text

```

They should not become a growth engine until economics are understood.

---

## 45. CAC Framework

Eventually:

```text
CAC
=
sales + marketing cost
/
new activated paying users
```

For VINSS, a more meaningful denominator may become:

```text
new users who complete a first paid deal.
```

Because:

```text
wallet connection
```

has little economic value by itself.

Compare CAC against:

```text
contribution from first deal
+
expected contribution from repeat deals.
```

---

## 46. LTV Should Not Be Invented Early

Do not calculate a confident lifetime value from:

```text
one month of usage
```

or:

```text
assumed multi-year retention.
```

Start with:

```text
30-day contribution;
60-day contribution;
90-day contribution.
```

Then extend only when observed retention supports it.

Avoid:

```text
100% anonymous;
invisible transactions;
zero metadata.
```

## 51. Refund and Dispute Messaging

## 70. GTM Principles

1. **Acquire real deals, not merely wallets.**
2. **Founder-led customer discovery comes before scaled acquisition.**
3. **The beachhead is discovered through repeated behavior.**
4. **Pricing must be tested on real payment decisions.**
5. **Subsidy should be visible and separated from normal pricing.**
6. **Grants extend runway; they do not validate demand.**
7. **Hackathons create visibility; they do not create PMF.**
8. **Community participation should produce learning, not spam.**
9. **The second participant is part of the acquisition problem.**
10. **A successful settlement is a stronger activation event than wallet connection.**
11. **Repeat deals matter more than first-time curiosity.**
12. **Organic counterparty-to-creator referral is a powerful market signal.**
13. **Token incentives must not lead product acquisition.**
14. **Loyalty should reward genuine downstream activity.**
15. **Partner integrations should follow demonstrated end-user value.**
16. **Security and clear privacy claims are GTM inputs.**
17. **Current capability and Target Design must never be blurred.**
18. **Scale only after contribution economics and retention are credible.**
19. **Kill weak segments instead of defending assumptions.**
20. **The final GTM engine should emerge from evidence, not from a launch checklist.**

## 71. GTM Thesis

The initial VINSS GTM loop should be:

```text
Find real direct deal
        ↓
Bring both parties into VINSS
        ↓
Create explicit agreement
        ↓
Complete protected settlement
        ↓
Learn
        ↓
User returns
        ↓
User invites next counterparty
```

If that loop works repeatedly inside one coherent segment, VINSS has the beginning of a market.

Only then should the company aggressively add:

```text
paid acquisition;
large partnerships;
token campaigns;
broader geographic expansion;
multiple verticals.
```

The objective is not to manufacture activity.

> **The objective is to make VINSS the workflow a user chooses again when the next real deal appears.**
